Step 5 of 5

Open the box

Make the walls transparent. Radiation can now leave the black hole into a bath, and the bath sends thermal radiation back in. The black hole stays in equilibrium, but its radiation keeps getting more entangled with what it left behind, until an island appears.

A black hole in a bath

Glue a flat, non-gravitating half-space to each boundary of the black hole of step 2, and let a two-dimensional conformal field theory with central charge ccc live on both. Its boundary conditions at the glue are transparent, so the walls of the box no longer reflect anything. Keep the bath at the black hole’s own temperature 1/β1/\beta1/β. This is the set-up of Almheiri, Mahajan and Maldacena. On the right side, with the boundary time ttt and a coordinate yyy that is negative in AdS and positive in the bath,

Hawking’s answer

Let R\mathbf RR be both baths beyond the point y=by=by=b, at boundary time ttt on each side. The state of everything is pure, so without islands the entropy of R\mathbf RR equals that of its complement: an interval from bbb on the left, through the wormhole, to bbb on the right. In a vacuum, for a metric ds2=−dw+dw−/Ω2ds^2=-dw^+dw^-/\Omega^2ds2=−dw+dw−/Ω2, the entropy of an interval is (Calabrese and Cardy)

with δ\deltaδ the short-distance cutoff. With (5.3) this gives

SHawking(t)=c3ln⁡[βπ δ εcosh⁡2πtβ],(5.6)S_{\rm Hawking}(t)=\frac{c}{3}\ln\left[\frac{\beta}{\pi\,\delta\,\varepsilon}\cosh\frac{2\pi t}{\beta}\right], \tag{5.6}SHawking​(t)=3c​ln[πδεβ​coshβ2πt​],(5.6)

which at late times grows linearly, forever:

SHawking≈2πc3 tβ.(5.7)S_{\rm Hawking}\approx\frac{2\pi c}{3}\,\frac{t}{\beta}. \tag{5.7}SHawking​≈32πc​βt​.(5.7)

As a function of time, (5.6) is exactly c/6c/6c/6 times the wormhole length (4.2), plus a constant, with tL=tR=tt_L=t_R=ttL​=tR​=t. The two baths become entangled through the growing wormhole, as in Hartman and Maldacena’s higher-dimensional calculation.

That is the information paradox in this model. The radiation keeps exchanging entangled quanta with the black holes, but its entropy cannot exceed what its complement can hold, about 2SBH=2(ϕ0+ϕh)/4G2S_{\rm BH}=2(\phi_0+\phi_h)/4G2SBH​=2(ϕ0​+ϕh​)/4G for the two black holes. Hawking’s answer breaks that bound after a time of order βSBH/c\beta S_{\rm BH}/cβSBH​/c.

The island

The gravitational path integral gives a different rule. When it is evaluated with the replica trick, new saddles appear, replica wormholes, that connect the copies through the black hole interior. They give the island formula:

S(R)=min⁡I ext⁡I[ ∑∂Iϕ0+ϕ4G+Smatter(R∪I)].(5.8)S(\mathbf R)=\min_{\mathbf I}\ \operatorname*{ext}_{\mathbf I}\left[\ \sum_{\partial\mathbf I}\frac{\phi_0+\phi}{4G}+S_{\rm matter}(\mathbf R\cup\mathbf I)\right]. \tag{5.8}S(R)=Imin​ Iext​[ ∂I∑​4Gϕ0​+ϕ​+Smatter​(R∪I)].(5.8)

Here I\mathbf II may be empty, which gives back (5.6). In JT gravity the “area” at each edge of the island is just the dilaton there, which is why everything in this step is computable. Take I\mathbf II to run from a point y=a<0y=a<0y=a<0 on the left, through the wormhole, to y=ay=ay=a on the right. At late times the two sides contribute separately, and each side gives

Sgen(a)=ϕ0+ϕ(a)4G+c6ln⁡sinh⁡2(π(b−a)/β)sinh⁡(2π∣a∣/β)+const.(5.9)S_{\rm gen}(a)=\frac{\phi_0+\phi(a)}{4G}+\frac{c}{6}\ln\frac{\sinh^2\big(\pi(b-a)/\beta\big)}{\sinh\big(2\pi|a|/\beta\big)}+\text{const}. \tag{5.9}Sgen​(a)=4Gϕ0​+ϕ(a)​+6c​lnsinh(2π∣a∣/β)sinh2(π(b−a)/β)​+const.(5.9)

The dilaton term wants the island edge deep, near the horizon, where ϕ\phiϕ is smallest. The matter term wants it close to bbb, so that less of the radiation’s partners are left outside. They balance at

sinh⁡(π(∣a∣−b)/β)sinh⁡(π(∣a∣+b)/β)=3πϕrG c β 1sinh⁡(2π∣a∣/β),∣a∣≈b+β2πln⁡6πϕrG c β,(5.10)\begin{aligned}&\frac{\sinh\big(\pi(|a|-b)/\beta\big)}{\sinh\big(\pi(|a|+b)/\beta\big)}=\frac{3\pi\phi_r}{G\,c\,\beta}\,\frac{1}{\sinh(2\pi|a|/\beta)},\\ &|a|\approx b+\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\ln\frac{6\pi\phi_r}{G\,c\,\beta},\end{aligned} \tag{5.10}​sinh(π(∣a∣+b)/β)sinh(π(∣a∣−b)/β)​=Gcβ3πϕr​​sinh(2π∣a∣/β)1​,∣a∣≈b+2πβ​lnGcβ6πϕr​​,​(5.10)

where the approximation holds for ϕr/4G≫cβ\phi_r/4G\gg c\betaϕr​/4G≫cβ. With 4G=14G=14G=1 these are Almheiri–Mahajan–Maldacena’s (23) and (24). The edge of the island is a finite distance in yyy from the boundary, so it lies outside the horizon, which is at y→−∞y\to-\inftyy→−∞. In the figure ϕ\phiϕ there is just above ϕh\phi_hϕh​. The island’s value, 2Sgen(a)2S_{\rm gen}(a)2Sgen​(a), does not change with time. So the entropy of the radiation is

and the two answers cross at the Page time,

tPage≈3β2πc ⋅2Sgen(a)≈3βπc SBH,(5.12)t_{\rm Page}\approx\frac{3\beta}{2\pi c}\,\cdot 2S_{\rm gen}(a)\approx\frac{3\beta}{\pi c}\,S_{\rm BH}, \tag{5.12}tPage​≈2πc3β​⋅2Sgen​(a)≈πc3β​SBH​,(5.12)

Most of the black hole interior, the shaded diamond, then belongs to the radiation: the fine-grained entropy of the radiation is computed by a region that includes the inside of the black hole.

What is exact here, and what is not

Exact: the geometry, the dilaton, the matter state and the single-interval entropies, within JT gravity with a conformal bath. The figure also finds the island edge by exact minimisation of (5.9).

Approximations:

  • In the island phase the two sides are treated as independent. This is accurate at late times, which is when the island dominates.
  • The semiclassical regime needs many matter fields, c≫1c\gg1c≫1, and a large black hole, ϕr/4G≫cβ\phi_r/4G\gg c\betaϕr​/4G≫cβ.

Not shown:

  • A black hole that actually evaporates into a colder bath (Almheiri, Engelhardt, Marolf and Maxfield; Almheiri, Mahajan, Maldacena and Zhao).
  • The replica wormholes themselves.
  • The algebraic side of the story, in which the entropy of the crossed-product algebra of observables equals the generalized entropy (Chandrasekaran, Penington and Witten).
