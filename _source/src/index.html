An interactive introduction to JT gravity

A Black Hole in a Box

Zoom into the throat of a charged black hole that is almost extremal and you find a two-dimensional spacetime, AdS₂, with one extra field attached: the area of the sphere you zoomed past. What is left of general relativity there is Jackiw–Teitelboim (JT) gravity. It is simple enough to solve exactly, and rich enough to have horizons, wormholes and Hawking radiation.

These pages build it up one step at a time. Every picture is drawn from an exact solution, and every step says where its equations come from, so you can check them.

Contents

  1. AdS₂ is a boxLive

    Free particles oscillate like masses on springs, all with the same period. Light reaches infinity in a finite time and comes back, and everything refocuses.

  2. Where is the black hole?Live

    The geometry never changes. The black hole lives in the dilaton and in where the boundary sits. Horizons, temperature, and signals that take forever to arrive.

  3. Throw something inComing in v0.2

    Matter leaves the geometry alone but makes the horizon jump, before the matter even arrives. Negative energy makes the wormhole traversable.

  4. Two sides, one wormholeComing in v0.2

  5. Open the boxLater

How to read the diagrams

Each diagram is a conformal (Penrose) diagram. Time runs up, light travels at 45°, and edges that are infinitely far away are drawn at a finite distance. Units are \(\hbar=c=k_B=1\), with the AdS radius \(\ell\) shown explicitly where it matters. The full set of conventions, and how they compare with the papers, is on the conventions page.
