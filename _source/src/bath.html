Step 5 of 5

Open the box

Make the walls transparent. Radiation can now leave the black hole into a bath, and the bath sends thermal radiation back in. The black hole stays in equilibrium, but its radiation keeps getting more entangled with what it left behind, until an island appears.

A black hole in a bath

Glue a flat, non-gravitating half-space to each boundary of the black hole of step 2, and let a two-dimensional conformal field theory with central charge \(c\) live on both. Its boundary conditions at the glue are transparent, so the walls of the box no longer reflect anything. Keep the bath at the black hole’s own temperature \(1/\beta\). This is the set-up of Almheiri, Mahajan and Maldacena. On the right side, with the boundary time \(t\) and a coordinate \(y\) that is negative in AdS and positive in the bath,

\[ ds^2=\frac{(2\pi/\beta)^2\,(-dt^2+dy^2)}{\sinh^2(2\pi y/\beta)}\quad(y<0),\qquad ds^2=\frac{-dt^2+dy^2}{\varepsilon^2}\quad(y>0), \tag{5.1} \] \[ \phi=\phi_h\coth\frac{2\pi|y|}{\beta},\qquad \phi_h=\frac{2\pi\phi_r}{\beta}\qquad(y<0). \tag{5.2} \]

This is the same black hole as in step 2, written with the tortoise coordinate \(y\) of (2.5): \(r=r_h\coth(r_h|y|)\). The horizon is at \(y\to-\infty\), and the boundary at \(y\to0^-\), where \(\phi\to\phi_r/|y|\). At equal temperatures, the energy the black hole radiates is exactly replaced by the bath, so nothing in (5.1)–(5.2) changes with time.

The matter is in the Hartle–Hawking state, which is thermal in \(t\). In the coordinates

\[ w^+=e^{2\pi(t+y)/\beta},\qquad w^-=-e^{-2\pi(t-y)/\beta} \tag{5.3} \]

it is simply the vacuum, and the whole two-sided spacetime, both baths included, is conformal to the flat \(w\)-plane. The link to the earlier diagrams is

\[ w^\pm=\tan\frac{\tau\pm\sigma}{2}, \tag{5.4} \]

with \((\tau,\sigma)\) the global coordinates of steps 1–4. Under (5.4) the dilaton (5.2) is exactly \(\phi_h\cos\tau/\cos\sigma\) again. The two AdS boundaries are the old edges \(\sigma=\pm\pi/2\), now open, and the baths are the triangles beyond them. The inner horizons of step 2 become the bath’s future null infinity, where outgoing radiation ends up.

Hawking’s answer

Let \(\mathbf R\) be both baths beyond the point \(y=b\), at boundary time \(t\) on each side. The state of everything is pure, so without islands the entropy of \(\mathbf R\) equals that of its complement: an interval from \(b\) on the left, through the wormhole, to \(b\) on the right. In a vacuum, for a metric \(ds^2=-dw^+dw^-/\Omega^2\), the entropy of an interval is (Calabrese and Cardy)

\[ S=\frac{c}{6}\ln\frac{-\Delta w^+\,\Delta w^-}{\delta^2\,\Omega_1\Omega_2}, \tag{5.5} \]

with \(\delta\) the short-distance cutoff. With (5.3) this gives

\[ S_{\rm Hawking}(t)=\frac{c}{3}\ln\left[\frac{\beta}{\pi\,\delta\,\varepsilon}\cosh\frac{2\pi t}{\beta}\right], \tag{5.6} \]

which at late times grows linearly, forever:

\[ S_{\rm Hawking}\approx\frac{2\pi c}{3}\,\frac{t}{\beta}. \tag{5.7} \]

As a function of time, (5.6) is exactly \(c/6\) times the wormhole length (4.2), plus a constant, with \(t_L=t_R=t\). The two baths become entangled through the growing wormhole, as in Hartman and Maldacena’s higher-dimensional calculation.

That is the information paradox in this model. The radiation keeps exchanging entangled quanta with the black holes, but its entropy cannot exceed what its complement can hold, about \(2S_{\rm BH}=2(\phi_0+\phi_h)/4G\) for the two black holes. Hawking’s answer breaks that bound after a time of order \(\beta S_{\rm BH}/c\).

The island

The gravitational path integral gives a different rule. When it is evaluated with the replica trick, new saddles appear, replica wormholes, that connect the copies through the black hole interior. They give the island formula:

\[ S(\mathbf R)=\min_{\mathbf I}\ \operatorname*{ext}_{\mathbf I}\left[\ \sum_{\partial\mathbf I}\frac{\phi_0+\phi}{4G}+S_{\rm matter}(\mathbf R\cup\mathbf I)\right]. \tag{5.8} \]

Here \(\mathbf I\) may be empty, which gives back (5.6). In JT gravity the “area” at each edge of the island is just the dilaton there, which is why everything in this step is computable. Take \(\mathbf I\) to run from a point \(y=a<0\) on the left, through the wormhole, to \(y=a\) on the right. At late times the two sides contribute separately, and each side gives

\[ S_{\rm gen}(a)=\frac{\phi_0+\phi(a)}{4G}+\frac{c}{6}\ln\frac{\sinh^2\big(\pi(b-a)/\beta\big)}{\sinh\big(2\pi|a|/\beta\big)}+\text{const}. \tag{5.9} \]

The dilaton term wants the island edge deep, near the horizon, where \(\phi\) is smallest. The matter term wants it close to \(b\), so that less of the radiation’s partners are left outside. They balance at

\[ \begin{aligned}&\frac{\sinh\big(\pi(|a|-b)/\beta\big)}{\sinh\big(\pi(|a|+b)/\beta\big)}=\frac{3\pi\phi_r}{G\,c\,\beta}\,\frac{1}{\sinh(2\pi|a|/\beta)},\\ &|a|\approx b+\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\ln\frac{6\pi\phi_r}{G\,c\,\beta},\end{aligned} \tag{5.10} \]

where the approximation holds for \(\phi_r/4G\gg c\beta\). With \(4G=1\) these are Almheiri–Mahajan–Maldacena’s (23) and (24). The edge of the island is a finite distance in \(y\) from the boundary, so it lies outside the horizon, which is at \(y\to-\infty\). In the figure \(\phi\) there is just above \(\phi_h\). The lower chart shows (5.9) as a function of \(|a|\), with its minimum at the island edge.

The island’s value, \(2S_{\rm gen}(a)\), does not change with time. So the entropy of the radiation is

\[ S(\mathbf R)=\min\Big[\,S_{\rm Hawking}(t),\ 2S_{\rm gen}(a)\Big], \tag{5.11} \]

and the two answers cross at the Page time,

\[ t_{\rm Page}\approx\frac{3\beta}{2\pi c}\,\cdot 2S_{\rm gen}(a)\approx\frac{3\beta}{\pi c}\,S_{\rm BH}, \tag{5.12} \]

up to terms that do not grow with \(S_{\rm BH}\). Before it, Hawking’s saddle dominates and there is no island. After it, the island saddle dominates and the entropy stays flat near \(2S_{\rm BH}\). Most of the black hole interior, the shaded diamond, then belongs to the radiation: the fine-grained entropy of the radiation is computed by a region that includes the inside of the black hole.

Derivations, and what was checked

From (5.3), \(dw^+dw^-=(2\pi/\beta)^2e^{4\pi y/\beta}dy^+dy^-\), so a metric \(-dy^+dy^-/\Omega_y^2\) becomes \(-dw^+dw^-/\Omega_w^2\) with \(\Omega_w=(2\pi/\beta)\,e^{2\pi y/\beta}\,\Omega_y\). Here \(\Omega_y=(\beta/2\pi)\sinh(2\pi|y|/\beta)\) in AdS and \(\varepsilon\) in the bath. For (5.6), the right point is \(w=(e^{2\pi(t+b)/\beta},-e^{-2\pi(t-b)/\beta})\) and the left one is \(w=(-e^{-2\pi(t-b)/\beta},e^{2\pi(t+b)/\beta})\), so \(-\Delta w^+\Delta w^-=4e^{4\pi b/\beta}\cosh^2(2\pi t/\beta)\), and both factors of \(\Omega_w\) equal \((2\pi/\beta)e^{2\pi b/\beta}\varepsilon\). For one side of (5.9), at equal times, \(-\Delta w^+\Delta w^-=4e^{2\pi(a+b)/\beta}\sinh^2(\pi(b-a)/\beta)\), which gives (5.9).

The cutoff at the island’s edges, \((c/6)\ln(1/\delta)\) per edge, is absorbed into \(\phi_0\); this is the usual renormalisation of Newton’s constant by matter. The constants left in (5.6) and (5.9) depend on how the cutoffs are chosen. They shift the Page time by an amount that does not grow with \(S_{\rm BH}\).

Checked numerically before publishing:

  • the map (5.4), the dilaton, and the metric factors;
  • (5.6) and (5.9) against a direct evaluation of (5.5);
  • the minimum of (5.9), found by direct minimisation, which satisfies Almheiri–Mahajan–Maldacena’s exact condition (their eq. 23) to seven digits.

Their normalisation is \(4G=1\), with a total dilaton \(\phi_{\rm AMM}=\phi_0+\phi\); see the table on the conventions page.

What is exact here, and what is not

Exact: the geometry, the dilaton, the matter state and the single-interval entropies, within JT gravity with a conformal bath. The figure also finds the island edge by exact minimisation of (5.9).

Approximations:

  • In the island phase the two sides are treated as independent. This is accurate at late times, which is when the island dominates.
  • The semiclassical regime needs many matter fields, \(c\gg1\), and a large black hole, \(\phi_r/4G\gg c\beta\).

Not shown:

  • A black hole that actually evaporates into a colder bath (Almheiri, Engelhardt, Marolf and Maxfield; Almheiri, Mahajan, Maldacena and Zhao).
  • The replica wormholes themselves.
  • The algebraic side of the story, in which the entropy of the crossed-product algebra of observables equals the generalized entropy (Chandrasekaran, Penington and Witten).
