Conventions

Everything on these pages uses the conventions below. Equations here are numbered (C.n) so the steps can refer to them, and numbers are never reused.

Units and signature

The signature is mostly plus, \((-,+)\), and \(\hbar=c=k_B=1\). \(\ell\) is the AdS radius; formulas marked \((\ell=1)\) set it to one. \(G\) is the two-dimensional Newton constant, so entropies such as \(\phi_0/4G\) are pure numbers.

Action and boundary conditions

\[ \begin{aligned} I={}&\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int_M d^2x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\Big[\phi_0R+\phi\Big(R+\frac{2}{\ell^2}\Big)\Big]\\ &+\frac{1}{8\pi G}\int_{\partial M}dt\,\sqrt{-\gamma}\,\Big[\phi_0K+\phi\Big(K-\frac{1}{\ell}\Big)\Big]+I_{\rm matter}[g,\chi]. \end{aligned} \tag{C.1} \]

The \(\phi_0\) term is topological: it does not affect the equations of motion, and in the Euclidean path integral it contributes \(S_0\chi\), with \(S_0=\phi_0/4G\) and \(\chi\) the Euler characteristic. The \(-\phi/\ell\) boundary term is the counterterm that keeps the action finite. Matter fields \(\chi\) couple to the metric but not to \(\phi\). At the boundary we fix

\[ \gamma_{tt}=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^2},\qquad \phi\big|_{\partial M}=\frac{\phi_r}{\varepsilon},\qquad \varepsilon\to0\qquad(\ell=1). \tag{C.2} \]

Equations of motion

\[ R=-\frac{2}{\ell^2}, \tag{C.3} \] \[ \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\phi-g_{\mu\nu}\nabla^2\phi+\frac{1}{\ell^2}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\phi=-8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}, \tag{C.4} \]

with \(T_{\mu\nu}=-(2/\sqrt{-g})\,\delta I_{\rm matter}/\delta g^{\mu\nu}\). (C.3) comes from varying \(\phi\) and (C.4) from varying \(g^{\mu\nu}\). In vacuum the trace of (C.4) gives \(\nabla^2\phi=2\phi/\ell^2\), and then \(\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\phi=g_{\mu\nu}\,\phi/\ell^2\).

Varying the metric

For any scalar \(f\), up to total derivatives,

\[ \delta\!\left(\sqrt{-g}\,fR\right)=\sqrt{-g}\left(f\,G_{\mu\nu}+g_{\mu\nu}\nabla^2f-\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu f\right)\delta g^{\mu\nu}, \tag{C.4a} \]

and \(G_{\mu\nu}\equiv0\) in two dimensions, which is why \(\phi_0R\) drops out. The cosmological term gives \(\delta\big(\sqrt{-g}\,2\phi/\ell^2\big)=-\sqrt{-g}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,(\phi/\ell^2)\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}\), and the matter gives \(-\tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\,T_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}\). Setting the sum to zero and multiplying by \(-16\pi G\) gives (C.4). Along a null direction \(k^\mu\), (C.4) says \(k^\mu k^\nu\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\phi=-8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu\): positive energy focuses \(\phi\), the two-dimensional version of the Raychaudhuri equation.

AdS₂

AdS₂ is (the universal cover of) the hyperboloid

\[ -T_1^2-T_2^2+X^2=-\ell^2\quad\text{in}\quad ds^2=-dT_1^2-dT_2^2+dX^2. \tag{C.5} \]

Global coordinates:

\[ \begin{aligned} &T_1=\frac{\ell\cos\tau}{\cos\sigma},\qquad T_2=\frac{\ell\sin\tau}{\cos\sigma},\qquad X=\ell\tan\sigma,\\ &ds^2=\frac{\ell^2}{\cos^2\sigma}\left(-d\tau^2+d\sigma^2\right). \end{aligned} \tag{C.6} \]

The two-sided black hole

\[ \phi=\phi_h\,\frac{T_1}{\ell}=\phi_h\,\frac{\cos\tau}{\cos\sigma}, \tag{C.7} \] \[ \begin{aligned} &ds^2=-(r^2-r_h^2)\,dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{r^2-r_h^2},\qquad \phi=\phi_r\,r,\\ &r_h=\frac{\phi_h}{\phi_r},\qquad \frac{r}{r_h}=\frac{\cos\tau}{\cos\sigma}\qquad(\ell=1). \end{aligned} \tag{C.8} \] \[ \beta=\frac{2\pi}{r_h}=\frac{2\pi\phi_r}{\phi_h},\qquad \tan\frac{\tau}{2}=\tanh\frac{\pi t}{\beta}\ \ \text{on each boundary}, \tag{C.9} \] \[ S=\frac{\phi_0+\phi_h}{4G},\qquad E=\frac{\phi_h^2}{16\pi G\,\phi_r}=\frac{\pi\phi_r}{4G\,\beta^2}. \tag{C.10} \]

Null coordinates and shock waves

With \(x^\pm=\tau\pm\sigma\), the metric (C.6) is \(ds^2=-e^{2\omega}dx^+dx^-\) with \(e^{2\omega}=\ell^2/\cos^2\sigma\), and the \(++\) component of (C.4) reads

\[ e^{2\omega}\,\partial_+\!\left(e^{-2\omega}\,\partial_+\phi\right)=-8\pi G\,T_{++}. \tag{C.11} \]

A pulse leaving the right boundary at global time \(\tau_s\) with boundary energy \(E_s\), \(T_{++}=p\,\delta(x^+-\tau_s-\pi/2)\), gives (step 3, \(\ell=1\))

\[ \begin{aligned}&\phi=\phi_h\frac{\cos\tau}{\cos\sigma}+\lambda\,\frac{\sin\sigma-\cos(\tau-\tau_s)}{\cos\sigma}\,\Theta\!\left(x^+-\tau_s-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right),\\ &\lambda=-8\pi G\,p=-\frac{8\pi G\,\phi_rE_s}{\phi_h\cos\tau_s},\end{aligned} \tag{C.12} \] \[ \phi_h'^2=\phi_h^2+16\pi G\,\phi_rE_s . \tag{C.13} \]

For any vacuum solution \(\phi=(AT_1+BT_2+CX)/\ell\) with \(A^2+B^2>C^2\), the right boundary’s clock runs at

\[ dt=\frac{\phi_r\,d\tau}{A\cos\tau+B\sin\tau+C}\qquad(\ell=1). \tag{C.14} \]

The wormhole length

\[ \begin{aligned}&\tilde L=d-2\ln\frac{2\phi_b}{\varepsilon}=2\ln\left[\frac{\cosh\big(\pi(t_L+t_R)/\beta\big)}{\Phi_h}\right],\\ &\phi_b=\frac{\phi_r}{16\pi G},\qquad \Phi_h=\frac{\phi_h}{16\pi G},\end{aligned} \tag{C.15} \] \[ \begin{aligned}&P=2\Phi_h\tanh\frac{\pi(t_L+t_R)}{\beta},\\ &H=H_L+H_R=\frac{P^2}{2\phi_b}+\frac{2}{\phi_b}e^{-\tilde L}=\frac{2\Phi_h^2}{\phi_b}.\end{aligned} \tag{C.16} \]
