Two sides, one wormhole

The two boundaries of the black hole are joined through the interior by a wormhole. Its length is the one thing pure JT gravity can do: it shrinks, bounces and grows, like a particle thrown at a wall.

Measuring the wormhole

Its length ddd diverges as ε→0\varepsilon\to0ε→0, because both boundaries are infinitely far away, so the divergence has to be subtracted. Only tL+tRt_L+t_RtL​+tR​ matters

The length depends on the two times only through their sum. That comes from a symmetry: the boost that leaves the dilaton (2.3) unchanged moves the right boundary forward in time and the left one backward. In pure JT the two boundary energies are always equal, HL=HRH_L=H_RHL​=HR​ (Harlow and Jafferis (2.20)), so evolving with HR−HLH_R-H_LHR​−HL​ does nothing to the state. Press Boost: the geodesic slides along, and its length stays fixed.

It grows forever

At late times cosh⁡x≈ex/2\cosh x\approx e^x/2coshx≈ex/2, so

At times of order eS0e^{S_0}eS0​, effects beyond the semiclassical approximation stop it (Iliesiu, Mezei and Sárosi).

Why matter changes everything

In pure JT, HL=HRH_L=H_RHL​=HR​ always. So the two-sided Hilbert space does not split into a left part and a right part; Harlow and Jafferis call this the factorisation problem. Matter changes that. The pulse of step 3 is the simplest state in which the two boundaries have different energies: afterwards the right boundary has E+EsE+E_sE+Es​ and the left still has EEE. With quantum matter fields, Penington and Witten show that the observables of one boundary, its Hamiltonian together with matter operators dressed to that boundary, form a Type II∞_\infty∞​ von Neumann algebra. That algebra has a trace, and therefore a well-defined entropy.
